# Literature coverage and reference verification

Use this for substantial drafting and before calling a paper or note ready.
For a local wording edit, preserve already checked citations unless the claim
or its scope changes.

## Build a literature discussion, not an isolated bibliography

Explain how the question arose, what the closest results establish, which
definition or method is inherited, and what the present argument adds. A
paragraph naming papers without comparing their results is not a literature
discussion. An introduction can carry this work in a short note; a separate
Related work section is useful when the comparisons need more space.

Cover these roles, allowing one source to serve several of them:

| Role | What to look for and cite |
| --- | --- |
| Origin of the question | The original problem or conjecture and its exact version/locator; relevant papers by the correspondent or authors whose question is answered |
| Definitions and conventions | Original sources for the borrowed definitions used here; standard sources for the chosen convention when variants exist |
| Tools and prior results | Primary sources for every theorem, construction, algorithm, or nontrivial method used in the argument, cited at the point of use |
| Standard theory | The field's relevant monographs and surveys, with the chapter or result that supplies context or a standard fact |
| Closest comparison | The strongest or nearest earlier results, with their domains and hypotheses compared explicitly to ours |
| Recent related work | Current preprints and published work on the same or neighboring question, including work that overlaps, anticipates, or supersedes an example |

Familiar elementary notation does not need a fabricated origin story. Borrowed
definitions, substantive background claims, and effective tools need sources.
Credit a correspondent's relevant papers on mathematical grounds; do not cite
irrelevant work out of courtesy. A survey helps discovery and context, but a
proof relying on an original theorem should identify that theorem's source.
Do not inherit a citation from another bibliography without checking it.

For a short paper, plan at least 12 substantively used, verified references,
normally 12–20 or more. This is a search and coverage target, not a reason to
pad the bibliography. A list of three or four papers should trigger another
search for missing standard theory, tools, and neighboring work. If a narrowly
focused 2–4 page note or venue constraint warrants fewer, record the completed
coverage search and reason in working notes. Do not describe the numeric target
as met. More than 20 is appropriate when the actual dependencies require it.

## Hard literature gate before calling a manuscript ready

Run an actual search beyond the source paper's own references. Record the
search date, queries or filters, sources consulted, and the papers that change
the comparison, attribution, or wording. Reuse an existing research log rather
than creating a parallel registry.

1. Search relevant arXiv categories or the search interface for roughly the
   last three years, using the problem's terminology, standard synonyms, and
   the names of the methods used.
2. Search MathSciNet or zbMATH for the problem, borrowed tools, and closest
   results. If an index is unavailable, inspect the relevant publisher pages
   and record which index/search remains unavailable.
3. Inspect Google Scholar citation results for the source paper when available,
   and follow relevant citing papers and references. Search the correspondent's
   related work and the relevant monographs or surveys directly.
4. Compare candidate overlaps by actual statements and proofs. A similar title
   is a lead, not evidence of either novelty or duplication.
5. Within one month of finalizing, refresh the search for newly posted work;
   inspect the last month's results for anything anticipating the headline
   theorem or example. Reopen any changed novelty comparison.

A bibliography citing only the source authors' papers fails this gate. When
available and authorized, obtain a separate referee or agent pass for missing
literature across the wider field and adjacent methods. Record an unavailable
independent pass honestly; do not call a self-review independent. A bounded
search supports a precise comparison, not a universal "first ever" assertion.

## Verify every entry and the sentence it supports

Use two checks for each reference. Neither replaces the other.

**Identity.** Check the entry against Crossref, arXiv, or zbMATH as appropriate.
Open the record; compare authors and spelling, title, year, journal or archive,
volume/pages or article number, DOI/identifier, and the version actually cited.
Use Crossref's DOI record for a journal article, arXiv's version history for a
preprint, and zbMATH plus publisher/catalogue information for mathematical
monographs or older papers. Check corrections, retractions, and later versions
where relevant. Resolve preprint/published duplicates rather than counting them
twice. Never manufacture a DOI for a book or repair metadata from memory.

**Support.** Open the source text and locate the definition, theorem, proof,
formula, or comparison that supports the cited sentence. Record the exact
locator in working notes and include it in the citation when needed. Match
domains, quantifiers, topology, regularity, boundedness, normalization, and
exceptional cases. A correct DOI, search snippet, abstract, or another model's
summary does not establish applicability of a theorem.

Where useful, keep a compact working table:

| Citation key | Identity record and checked date | Source locator | Claim supported and hypothesis match | Issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not put this verification table in the manuscript by default. Cite each
source where its contribution is used, and check that bibliography entries
are cited and in-text citations resolve. A single citation may support several
explicitly identified facts; a long undifferentiated citation cluster is weak
attribution.

If an index is inaccessible or has no record, record the attempted lookup and
use a primary publisher or library record to resolve identity where possible.
Report the outstanding index check; do not call it index-verified. If the
underlying source passage cannot be read, obtain it, remove the dependent
claim, or prove what is needed locally. Never fill the gap with an invented
entry or mark the manuscript fully checked. Independent supported writing
can continue while the specific source issue is resolved.

## Make the contribution affirmative

Write the comparison as prior result plus present difference: the earlier
domain, the hypotheses, and the new conclusion or proof step. Replace "we do
not claim that this method is new" with the method's citation and a precise
statement of the contribution. Replace a stack of "to the best of our
knowledge" caveats with a checked comparison; if priority cannot be supported,
drop the priority superlative and keep the mathematical result.

Reference count and recency are coverage diagnostics. Every retained citation
must earn its place by supporting a sentence, method, or comparison. Do not
copy a template's suggested reference list into a paper without checking it.
